## An invitation to build this in public

Urban Heat Democratization is ready for careful operational partnership—not for
uncritical adoption. Community members, mentors, public agencies, educators,
researchers, designers, engineers, and local organizations can help turn an
inspectable research workflow into a more useful, accountable civic tool.

## How you can help

| If you are… | You can help by… | What a good contribution produces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A resident or community advocate | Testing whether map interpretations match lived conditions; naming missing places, barriers, and priorities | A documented correction, question, or local context record |
| A public-health, planning, or climate practitioner | Reviewing decision boundaries, local data sources, and safe operational uses | A scoped pilot question and review protocol |
| A mentor or researcher | Stress-testing graph assumptions, sensitivity, uncertainty, reproducibility, and evaluation design | A method review, test, or reproducible analysis note |
| An engineer, GIS specialist, or data steward | Improving source provenance, update workflows, accessibility, privacy, and data contracts | A traceable, maintainable implementation improvement |
| An educator or student | Using the worked example and reporting where explanation is unclear | A clearer learning artifact or documented usability insight |
| A funder or public institution | Supporting a bounded, community-reviewed pilot and independent evaluation | A transparent pilot with public learning, not a procurement promise |

## The first operational pilot

Start small. Choose one public-interest question, one bounded geography, one
local partner group, and one accountable decision owner. Pre-specify what the
spectral layer may inform, what it may not decide, which inputs must be checked,
how residents can challenge the result, and what evidence would count as a
successful or unsuccessful pilot.

## The standard for participation

Please contribute evidence, criticism, or capacity—not a request to overstate
the model. Every operational use should preserve provenance, invite local
challenge, protect privacy, state uncertainty, and publish what changed after
review.

## Start here

1. Read the [worked graph-theory example](08-graph-theory-worked-example.md).
2. Review the [Spectral Theory Contract](09-spectral-theory-contract.md) and
   [Repeatability and Real-World Validation](10-repeatability-and-real-world-validation.md).
3. Open an issue, share a documented local source, propose a review, or contact
   [Aarti S Ravikumar](https://ai-aarti.com) to discuss a bounded partnership.

The invitation is simple: help make the science more locally truthful, the
workflow more usable, and any public action more accountable.
