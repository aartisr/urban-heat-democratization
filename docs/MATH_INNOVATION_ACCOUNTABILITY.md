# Mathematical Innovation Accountability Report

**Scope:** the equations and methods catalogued in [the complete urban thermal math guide](urban_thermal_math_the_complete_guide.md), traced against the code and user-facing application as of 2026-10-17. Advanced methods are implemented as reusable, manifest-gated primitives in [`core/advanced_thermal_math.py`](../core/advanced_thermal_math.py) and assurance controls in [`core/assurance.py`](../core/assurance.py); availability of a calculation does not waive its data, validation, or governance release gate.

## Executive finding

The app's distinctive mathematical contribution is **not a new satellite-temperature equation**. It is an auditable translation of a thermal/vegetation raster into a weighted spatial graph, then into three inspectable decision-support signals:

```text
LST (+ optional NDVI) raster
  → robustly normalized valid cells
  → weighted 4/8-neighbour graph
  → normalized-Laplacian / Fiedler sweep bottleneck
  → least-cost connection to inferred cooling sinks
  → stress-test and clearly labelled planning proxies
```

This makes a difficult question—*where does the model see a fragile thermal-landscape connection?*—visible, reproducible, and challengeable. It adds value by preserving the assumptions and the intermediate structural measures rather than collapsing them into an unexplained “hotspot score.” It is innovative in product design and integration: established spectral-graph, shortest-path, Monte Carlo, and optimization methods are combined with thermal imagery, exported map artifacts, a public learning lab, and explicit limits on interpretation.

It must **not** be described as a validated heat-flow simulator, an air-temperature model, a health-risk predictor, a cooling-centre accessibility model, or proof that an intervention will lower a location’s temperature. The advanced framework makes these methods available when a city package meets its release contract; it does not turn a generic formula into a locally validated claim.

## Reading the status labels

- **Product-active:** the method currently powers a shipped pipeline, API, or visible interface.
- **Preview-mapped:** the method has a visible but off-by-default, computationally verified map layer; it is not a public finding.
- **Framework-ready:** reusable, tested code exists, but no registered city artifact or public UI claim uses it yet.
- **Research-only:** requires the optional research environment and further diagnostics before it can produce a releasable artifact.
- **Externally gated:** a valid computation exists, but field data, governance, or an independent validation record is still required.

## Where a person encounters the math

- **City detail / atlas:** the imported Cheeger bottleneck and cooling-access artifacts make the raster-graph result inspectable on a map. The production pipeline ties the sequence together at [`core/pipeline.py`](../core/pipeline.py#L538-L605).
- **Spatial-pattern preview:** for a bundled thermal source, the city-map adapter produces an FDR-adjusted Local Moran/LISA categorical layer. Find it in **Boston → Explore the atlas → full-screen map → Layers and evidence → Fine-tune individual layers → Spatial pattern preview**. It is hidden by default, and its provenance panel says it is derived, single-scene, and computationally verified only.
- **Robustness Lab (`/robustness`):** exposes `λ₂`, sweep conductance, percolation, and sink reliability on a deliberately synthetic nine-node district. The endpoint is explicit about this scenario in [`api/main.py`](../api/main.py#L1215-L1318).
- **Scenario planner (`/scenarios`):** applies the exact/weighted budget math and labels its temperature, uncertainty, and confidence measures as proxies. It should be read as planning comparison, not a forecast.
- **Exports and reports:** map overlays, GeoTIFF/GeoJSON and figures preserve artifacts outside the interface. This is central to the innovation: a stakeholder can inspect the derived field instead of relying solely on a dashboard claim.

## What is genuinely innovative—and what is not

### The innovation

1. **Thermal imagery becomes a network question.** Standard LST/NDVI maps show intensity. This method also asks where the declared spatial field has weak modeled connections, then distinguishes that from modeled linkage to cooling-like sinks.
2. **The model is layered rather than monolithic.** Thermal gradient, vegetation, graph topology, spectral separation, access proxy, and stress tests are retained as separable concepts. A place can be a bottleneck, have poor modeled cooling access, both, or neither.
3. **Inspectable uncertainty is part of the interaction.** The app exposes source/evidence categories, fixed-seed stress assumptions, relative scaling, and proxy labels. That is a meaningful innovation over a black-box “AI recommendation” interface.
4. **One metric contract serves analysis and learning.** [`core/robustness_metrics.py`](../core/robustness_metrics.py#L1-L44) is shared by the pipeline and teaching surface, reducing the risk that the explainer demonstrates different mathematics from the implementation.
5. **Planning math is kept separate from physical claims.** Exact budget arithmetic and scenario proxy scoring can aid a transparent early conversation without being represented as construction optimization or measured cooling impact.

### Not a novelty claim

Laplacians, Fiedler vectors, Cheeger-style sweeps, Dijkstra, bond percolation, Monte Carlo reliability, and knapsack are established methods. The defensible novelty is their **transparent, equity-conscious and map-to-action integration**, not invention of those mathematical fields. Any research, grant, or publication statement should say “applies/combines” rather than “invents,” unless a future validation study establishes a new method and comparative performance.

## Claim language that stays accurate

Use: “The app derives **relative, graph-based bottleneck and cooling-linkage proxies** from prepared LST and optional NDVI rasters, then exposes their assumptions for local validation.”

Avoid: “The app proves where heat is trapped,” “predicts residents’ exposure,” “optimizes the best intervention,” or “calculates true temperature from raw satellite pixels.”
