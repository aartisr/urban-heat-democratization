## Status

## Purpose

Test whether neighborhood-scale evidence and local observation can help a
Boston community investigate one bounded cooling-access question. The pilot is
not a property assessment, health-risk tool, or intervention recommender.

## Pilot question template

> In **[bounded public geography]**, does the bundled neighborhood-scale
> evidence reveal a possible cooling-access or shade-continuity question that
> local observers and the accountable steward agree is worth investigating?

The question must name what the project may inform, what it cannot decide, and
the public action that could follow a validated finding (for example, a site
visit, shade audit, maintenance review, or feasibility study).

## Review roles

- **Community steward:** determines whether the question matters locally and
  can pause publication or request context.
- **Decision owner:** can act on a bounded request and report what happened.
- **Data steward:** verifies source, date, spatial scale, license, and update
  path for every layer.
- **Methods reviewer:** challenges graph construction, sensitivity, and the
  boundary between a signal and a conclusion.
- **Accessibility and privacy reviewer:** checks language, map alternatives,
  location handling, and the ability to participate without sharing an address.

One person may hold more than one role only when the potential conflict is
visible and the community steward agrees.

## Feedback and disagreement path

Every pilot result must invite these three responses:

1. **This matches the place.** Add local evidence that supports the question.
2. **This misses something.** Name the missing condition, source, or lived
   context without exposing personal or residential data.
3. **This should not be used here.** Request suppression, correction, or a
   revised interpretation.

Feedback is a review input, not a vote on the truth of a model. The project
must publish how feedback changed—or did not change—the evidence record.

## Ground-truth and equity protocol

Before showing a pilot spectral panel, pre-register:

- observation dates and safe, non-identifying collection rules;
- the locally meaningful conditions to inspect (for example shade continuity,
  public access, crossing safety, cooling-resource hours, or tree condition);
- comparison of model signals with observations, including disagreements;
- sensitivity checks for graph extent, adjacency, weights, sink rule, and
  source date;
- review for who could be burdened, excluded, stigmatized, or harmed by a
  public map or recommendation; and
- a decision to publish, revise, suppress, or sunset the result.

## Explicit exclusions

The pilot must not collect a participant’s home address, medical information,
immigration status, rent status, or other personal data unless a separately
approved protocol makes it necessary. It must not support enforcement,
eligibility, insurance, lending, rent-setting, or other adverse decisions.
