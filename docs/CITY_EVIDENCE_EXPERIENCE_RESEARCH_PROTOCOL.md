# City Evidence Experience: Moderated Research Protocol

> Use this protocol to test the guided city brief before treating its language,
> interaction, or comprehension as validated.

## Purpose

Evaluate whether people can distinguish a city finding from its evidence limits,
find methods/provenance, and move to map exploration or planning without
overinterpreting a derived priority.

## Participants

Recruit 5–6 people for each qualitative round, across:

- community/civic users without specialist mapping experience;
- city or planning practitioners; and
- educators, researchers, or policy analysts.

Run a second round after resolving high-severity findings. Do not treat the
first round as a statistical study; it is a way to find and correct major
comprehension failures.

## Session structure (30–45 minutes)

1. Ask the participant to think aloud while opening a bundled city.
2. Do not explain the Read / Explore / Audit labels before their first use.
3. Ask the tasks below in order. Record completion, hesitation, wrong turns,
   and exact language that signals overclaiming.
4. End with confidence-calibration questions, not just preference questions.

## Tasks

1. In one sentence, explain what the city page is saying and what it cannot
   establish.
2. Use the guided brief to identify whether the city has validated local study
   evidence or is awaiting inputs.
3. Explain the difference between an observed input and a derived priority.
4. Open Explore, locate a higher-priority area, and describe what would need
   further local investigation.
5. Open Audit and find the method, source/date, and one limitation.
6. Decide whether a scenario is appropriate now; explain why.
7. Repeat tasks 1–3 using an upload-first city and verify that no local finding
   is inferred from the city name or boundary.

## Success signals

- The participant describes a priority area as a prompt for investigation, not
  a temperature forecast or policy decision.
- They can find a limitation without being told where Audit is.
- They understand that incomplete cities receive an onboarding/readiness story
  rather than an absence of product value.
- They can use Back/Forward and shared URLs without losing orientation.

## Severity rubric

| Severity | Definition | Required response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | Participant makes a harmful or scientifically false inference. | Fix before release. |
| High | Participant cannot complete a core evidence, limitation, or provenance task. | Fix and retest. |
| Medium | Participant completes the task with material hesitation or incorrect detour. | Prioritize in the next iteration. |
| Low | Preference or minor wording concern with no comprehension impact. | Record and review. |

## Decision gate

Do not move from prototype to final guided-map implementation until no critical
misinterpretations remain and the content/method owner approves any changed
public-language claim or caveat.
