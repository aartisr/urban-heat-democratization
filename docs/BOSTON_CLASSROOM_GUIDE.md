# Boston Classroom Guide

Created by [Aarti S Ravikumar](https://ai-aarti.com/) for [Urban Heat Democratization](https://urban-heat.ai-aarti.com/).

This teaching companion helps students inspect the bundled Boston study without treating a map or model as a public-health conclusion. Begin with the [Boston study](https://urban-heat.ai-aarti.com/cities/boston) and its [source guide](BOSTON_STUDY_GUIDE.md).

## A classroom sequence

1. Locate the study boundary and identify what area it covers.
2. Compare the displayed thermal and cooling-access overlays. Ask what each source measures, when it was collected, and where it has gaps.
3. Inspect a graph bottleneck as a property of the documented model. Discuss which edge weights and adjacency choices could change the result.
4. Try a starter scenario and list the assumptions behind its budget and intervention rankings.
5. Write one question that needs local observations or community input before a public decision.

## What the evidence can support

The repository contains a Boston boundary and documented local overlays for inspection. A bottleneck identifies a weak connection in the chosen weighted graph; it does not by itself show where heat is trapped. A low-cooling-access layer is an analytical signal, not a finding about any person’s exposure or health. Scenario outputs are exploratory benchmarks, not validated Boston-specific temperature or equity predictions.
